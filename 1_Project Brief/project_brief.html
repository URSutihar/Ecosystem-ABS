Project Brief

Ecosystem ABM — Generalists vs Specialists · Project 16

40.015 Simulation Modeling & Analysis

Context & Problem Statement

Many decisions in our lives are about what is the optimal step to take in any situation. This is the fundamental research question in some game theoretic frameworks and generalizes very well in other domains. We want to know what does mathematics have to say about investing in a market, developing vaccines to diseases or training LLMs for any task.
Our project aims to answer this question by looking at it through the lens of a planet and its ecosystems. The uncertainty in predation success, reproduction, mortality, etc. model the systematic risks and environmental conditions and shocks model the idisyncratic risks.
Our simulation compares how generalist and specialists strategies affect species survival and ecosystem stability.
The model is grounded in Lotka–Volterra dynamics — one of the most important mathematical frameworks in population ecology — extended into a stochastic, agent-based setting with spatial heterogeneity across five distinct biomes.

Project Objective

To demonstrate, via Monte Carlo simulation, that diversifying away individual risks is a better strategy for long-term success under large-scale shocks as losses in some area are offset by gains in other, while going all-in on a particul seemingly winning strategy can backfire if it is no longer valid due to environmental changes or shocks.
We show this by modeling Generalist Predator (less wins diversified portfolio) vs Specialists Predator (more wins individual assets) and simulating different situations of systematic and idiosyncratic shocks.

Key Questions

  • Does generalist strategy have higher survival rates than specialist stratgey?
  • If human becomes an interstellar species, how should they approach and alien planet?
  • How do environmental shocks affect predator and prey survival dynamics?
  • Does Lotka-Volterra Model hold for multiple prey and predator species?

Stakeholders & Users

  • Ecologists & biology researchers — study ecosystem behaviour under stochastic shocks and compare generalist vs specialist trade-offs.
  • Academia — teach and learn ecology, predator–prey dynamics, agent-based modelling, stochastic modeling, Monte Carlo simulation methods and Lotka-Volterra framework
  • Astrobiology — understand the nature and behaviour of life forms to look for in extra-terrestial life hunt.
  • Game & environment designers — build believable, balanced ecosystems with emergent dynamics.

Cross-Domain Generalisation

Finance — Diversification & Portfolio Theory
A diversified portfolio (generalist) outperforms concentrated bets (specialist) under market shocks. Core insight of Modern Portfolio Theory (Markowitz); foundational to CAPM and APT.
Epidemiology
Broad-spectrum immunity (generalist) vs pathogen-specific defences (specialist).
Supply Chain
Multi-source procurement (generalist) vs single-supplier dependency (specialist).
Urban Planning
Mixed-use districts (generalist) vs single-industry towns (specialist).

This simulation illuminates the fundamental trade-off between efficiency and resilience — a structure recurring across ecology, finance, epidemiology, and systems engineering — through interactive, data-driven Monte Carlo analysis.
